title: The Young Mind.

Tonight is going to be forgettable. The last thing James wants to do is party, but Luca convinces him. Luca wants to make memories tonight, and James tags along. James lives passively, getting tossed by life’s waves. Staying in is predictable, anyway. They end up somewhere James has never been before, but the way it is doesn't surprise him. He hasn't been in this house, sure, but he’s been in the houses in this area. All the time he’s spent driving, biking, or walking by cookie cutter neighborhoods in his town, thinking his eyes are playing tricks with him, copy and pasting the same house over and over again: each a backyard apart. The only visible variation from the road is the color of doormat and front window curtains. He’s never been to the house he’s going to tonight, sure, but how different is this from the house next door?

James keeps his eyes in front of him. He vaguely acknowledges a familiar set of ceiling lights and the off-the-shelf marble of a kitchen counter. The party sounds muffled in James’ ears like he’s wearing headphones. He hardly sees who’s in front of him because their component parts make up the physicality of plenty of other humans. He thinks of the insignificance of getting to know anyone. That person might well live in a cookie cutter house and live a cookie cutter life. Maybe James fits in here, normal oozing out of the walls. It feels wrong to him to deny individuality. How would society advance without innovation? But society encourages thinking like the group, giving an answer to a question that’s both politically correct and polite. An expected answer, with its own doormat variation.

We’re so alike, and yet we can’t seem to connect with those around us. So, James keeps his eyes in front of him. Conversation is mechanical. It’s the same.

Time passes. Uneventful, time-wasting time. He’s out with people, distant from people, thinking about the same unanswerable, unchangeable and uncontrollable topics he would be in bed with the lights off.

He swims around in his convoluted thoughts as the world goes on around him. The people around him may as well be on a television screen.  His eyes merely film the blur of teenage mediocrity. He’s numb, but it helps ease the stress of existence.

James’ reality-screen brightness dims consistently. There’s a competitive nature in the room. It radiates into James, making him fidgety and anxious. For a moment, he has an indifferent clarity contrasted by enthusiastic immaturity sparking around the room. He looks around and studies behaviors. Everyone’s trying to be someone.

Instantaneously he feels grounded; he’s right in the middle of himself. He doesn’t know what to do with his hands. How do humans casually hold their arms? How are his peers standing, what are they wearing, does his outfit suit him in this setting? Does he have anything on his face? Does his hair look okay?

None of his senses work properly. He scans the crowd for Luca, thinking he’d better get home soon. Luca is nowhere to be seen. James passes through the party, unhinged from the passing. He looks for the confident mindset his best friend provides for him.

The world moves in slow motion. Laughter sounds like wind blowing in his ears, and he walks back through the same tall door frame, back through the same dark, wood-lined room. Everything’s in two dimensions in front of him.

Where’s Luca?

A hush falls over the party, or maybe he imagines it.

People glance up, and James follows their eyes to the second floor. Furrowed eyebrows, and a girl stomps down the stairs. Eyes wide, she looks almost like a painting, her shadow reflecting off the wall behind her. She doesn’t yell, hardly speaks, and notices everyone's attention trained on her.

Why is the reality-show paused?

Ripples of conversation ping pong through the room and reach him. A game of telephone from the wide-eyed girl to the people closest to him.

He knows where Luca is.

And then Luca hurdles down the stairs. He reaches the bottom. The record catches for a moment. The entire party holds their breath to see what Luca does next. And Luca does nothing. He goes to the bathroom and conversations continue. People forget about it. James is alone again, but he doesn’t have the same things on his mind.

The painting on the wall mocks him. People’s previously simplistic topics of conversation feel malicious to James as he stumbles about. He notices Luca slip out of the bathroom and down a hallway. James follows him.

A heavy door slams behind James. Silence follows.

This might be the first time in James’ years of knowing him that Luca isn’t moving at all. There’s always something. He’s either bouncing an ankle up and down, twisting a pencil between his fingers, answering questions before people finish asking them, or solving a Rubik’s cube during conversation. The world moves too slow for Luca, but tonight he deflates into a broken camp chair too small for him and takes swigs out of a bottle of Patron. The concrete floors ooze icy flavors into the air and there’s something unnerving about being able to see every inch of the floor and walls in a space usually filled with cars, storage, or whatever else. This two-car garage has nothing but frigid temperatures and two cubbies of camp gear. And Luca and James.

“Hey.” James mutters.

Luca grunts as a response and motions to another camp chair in a cubby on the wall.

“Are you alright?”

Luca hardly reacts to this question. He looks at, or past, the garage door and takes a swig of Patron. Luca passes the bottle to James when he gets set up in a camp chair, who takes it. They sit in silence. Music and muffled conversation seep through the cracks in the doorframe to the main house.

They sit there for a long while, or perhaps it’s only a minute, before Luca speaks. “You know how hard I’ve been trying to be happy, James?” He doesn’t look at James, he sits looking at nothing in front of him, and James wonders if he should say anything. He debates until Luca carries on. “I’m exhausted James. I’m constantly battling my mind because I know nothing I can ever think will be true objectively. I come to a conclusion about something and then it gets completely derailed by my next thought. My next thought provides me an equally valid solution but also completely disagrees with the thought prior.

“Why is it so damn exhausting to try? Why is it so much easier to fall into myself and just let the world carry me along? My chaotic brain is a curse. The best thing in the world that anyone can be is a beautiful fool.” Luca exhales and smirks, “My last strategy: enjoying the company of a beautiful fool.” Luca takes another swig of Patron, drinking himself to a thick haze.

Luca exhale laughs and raises one side of his mouth. He side-eyes James for a moment and his expression changes from humored to grief stricken. Luca’s mind goes so fast. He returns his glazed eyes to nowhere in particular, "I’m stuck in this three-dimensional world living with three-dimensional beings. My brain is overflowing. Reality is incapable of expressing what I feel. I wish I had more dimensions to work with. I’m so frustrated with people being the simplest versions of who I think them to be. There’s never any content. What-what’s the fucking point?” James nods, trying to see Luca’s words clearly through his blurry lens.

“I have no idea.” James pauses, wondering if he should continue. He relaxes into the silence instead, letting Luca’s mind go where it wants to.

The garage expands around them in silence. It morphs from a 200 square foot empty space to a world swimming with thoughts, doubts, and expectations, and James can feel Luca’s mind vibrating with opposing and contradictory ideas. James feels Luca’s confusion, and his frustration with his confusion. Sometimes you communicate more when sitting in silence with someone than talking. Luca peels his eyes away from the garage door and looks at James. He smirks at James playfully and takes another sip of the Patron. James cocks his head.

“I’ll drink until three dimensions feels like enough.”

The silence hums back into James’ ears, and the bottle of Patron dwindles as bubbles float up from Luca’s gulps. James remembers where they are with a burst of laughter from the main house reverberating under the garage door. James hears far off bass boosted music he forgot about.

Luca studies the patterns in the white painted wood and the metal skeleton along the edge. He observes the hinges holding the panels together and the rollers on the edge allowing the door to slide up and down with the click of a button. “I want to be seen.” He says.
