“Skip class. Come hang with me.”

James splutters; Luca’s eyes narrow.  “I don’t like skipping class. Can’t we just hang out after school?”

Luca sighs sarcastically. “No, James, we can’t quote ‘hang out after school’ unquote.” He did the thing with his hands too, “Do you realize how much time there is in a day? Do you realize how much precious life-living is wasted because of this prison, James? I have plenty of life-living to do, James. All the time I spend outside of this shithole is spent conquering and shredding and living. Living, James. You know what I do when I’m here? I twiddle my thumbs and look outside while some washed-out fuck gives me tasks to keep me busy.” James thinks Luca’s too whiney, but Luca continues anyway.

“I’m required by law to sit in a poorly built bureaucratic building by people who assume they know more about the world than me without any proper evidence. The only reason they think they have evidence is that they got some piece of paper from another poorly built bureaucratic building. They got a degree. They aged. They got a taste of power. Now they use it to reprimand me for getting to class late, and demand that I take an opinion on something I don’t care about. So, James, stop letting the bureaucratic power fiends feed off you and come live with me. Come live, James.”

James puts the last container back in his lunchbox and looks at Luca. “Everything sounds bad when you say it like that, Luca. To ski you attach long sticks to your feet and go down.”

Luca raises his eyes at James and then nods. “Fine, you got me. It was certainly a reductive perspective. So… you aren’t skipping with me?” He looks hopeful, side-eyeing James while throwing his backpack over his shoulder. James tilts his head back and forth contemplatively and then the bell rings, triggering the school cop to round a bend and stare down the stragglers in the cafeteria. James makes eye contact with the cop. His eyes dart to the ceiling to see a poster with a cartoon of a kid holding a lunchbox, and a title reading ‘lunchroom rules’.

James rolls his eyes. “Yeah. I’m coming. Fuck this poorly built bureaucratic building.”

Luca smiles across his face. The look of glee in his rich blue eyes spreads to James' face too, and they go out the side door in the cafeteria. James feels the cop's eyes burn holes in the back of his head. “Christ, Luca, how do you do this all the time?” He whispers only once they’re out the door.

“Can you relax a little, James? I do this shit all the time because I’m good at it. That cop won’t do shit.” The crisp air feels good on James’ face. “He’s the human equivalent of security cameras that don’t work, James.” The sun reflects off snow beside the crunchy sidewalk. “Non-working security cameras are acceptable though. As long as there’s minimal costs. It sparks fear, James.” James catches fire in Luca’s eyes. “Well, now I’m thinking they’re a waste of time. I’d steal shit anyway and then what?” Luca pauses and frosty white pine trees sprinkle over the rolling hills behind him. Luca turns around where James is looking. “Way better view than Mr. Carter’s ass crack, right, James?”

James snorts and nods at the icy path that drops to asphalt. The parking lot is eerily void of people like the ski lodge is in summer. At the far corner, tucked between two trees and a suburban, Luca’s mom’s yellow Hummer chirps when Luca points the keys in the air. “What are we doing?” James asks.

Luca holds a hooded expression as he rounds the car to start the engine. James hops into the passenger seat and slams the door. He buckles his seatbelt as Luca connects to aux. Luca clears his throat, “I thought I already told you what we were doing, James.” James doesn’t recall. “We’re living.”

 James shakes his head and exhales sharply. “That gives me no information.”

Luca laughs as he blasts rap on the speakers. He puts his hand on the back of James’ seat and looks backwards as he backs up. “Okay, so.” His glance seems humored towards James. He puts the Hummer in drive. “I don’t have a plan. I just have cigs, and I was gonna drive to a place I like to smoke.” He looks at James’ deadpan face and exaggerates his grimace. “Glad you’re coming along.”

James smiles when he looks out at the white landscape in front of them. “Yeah, this is better than Mr. Carter’s ass crack. Let’s go smoke some cigs.”

The massive hummer tires chomp through the icy road, and James notes how recklessly Luca drives down their residential streets. The sun reflects off rooftop snow, and James wishes he had sunglasses for the sting in his eyes. Luca drapes one hand over the steering wheel and taps his thumb along to the beat of his song. He raps the lyrics lazily as he puts on his blinker. The Hummer turns slowly onto a cold-crusted, one-way dirt road rolling out on the hills like a fruit roll up. They drop down a drooping hill into a valley of sagebrush with a moth-bitten blanket of crusted snow. Luca rolls down his window on the muddy terrain. Cold rushes in. James breathes deeply and feels freedom through the crisp wind against his face. Luca lolls a hand around out the window. He captures the wind too. James decides they’re thinking the same thing.

They whirr over a roller and enter a pond-filled valley. The water at the surface freezes over near the edge but chips closer to the middle. It looks like a giant put a finger against the surface to see if it would crunch. (It did).

James hums along to the base against his legs. Neither of them speak as Luca pulls into a turnaround point in the road. It overlooks the pond, and he shuts off the engine. What a strange car, James thinks. Painted along the black interior, hints of yellow reflect the sun. Luca grabs his backpack from the back, so James focuses on finding the door handle. When he finds it, it crunches open like the crunching snow under his feet. Luca fishes a lighter out of the backpack on his lap.

James likes the word crunch. He thinks it’s his new favorite word when he looks around at the crunchy snow over crunchy ice over crunchy plants. Luca walks in front of James rummaging with cigarette packaging. Crunch, crunch, crunch. He pulls out a cigarette and puts it between his lips. James follows in his crispy footsteps and crunchy cigarette packaging.

Ahead of him, Luca blows out a billow of smoke and laughs as it rises into the sky. One ha. A sardonic laughter. He’s motionless, gazing at the smoke rising into the air. James catches up to him while the wisps of smoke from his exhalation dissipate into the atmosphere. Luca leisurely scans from the sky down to James’ face and passes the cigarette. James takes it. “Do you have big thoughts about spirituality, James?” Luca asks.

James takes a pull from the cigarette, coughs, and tries to respond, “Yes.” Cough. “No.” Cough, cough. “I don’t know.” Pause. “My family’s technically Christian.” Cough‌, cough.

Luca noticeably rolls his eyes. “Yeah, James, I know your family is technically Christian. But I’m asking if you actually believe.” Luca’s walking in front of him again. Nature is quiet in the snow: it only crunches; it’s only crispy.

James flicks ash into the bushes by the edge of the pond. “I don’t think so. There’s so many questions that religion doesn’t have answers to.” The path in front of them fills their vision and James watches the rocky, crumbly surface.

“Hmm.” Luca stops abruptly and James almost runs into him. He faces James, and holds his hand out for the cigarette. James turns it around in his fingers and hands it over. Luca takes a pull as he turns around, and the path fills their vision again. “I feel like they always have answers, just not satisfying ones.” Luca breathes out a stream of smoke. “They have answers determined from when they thought the earth was flat and the center of the universe. I feel like our moral standards should evolve like our minds.”

James furrows his eyebrows at Luca’s crunching footprints, and squints at the reflective white edges of the path, “Man, Christianity doesn’t even admit physical evolution. No chance would they consider mental evolution.”

“Hmm.” Luca says again, but then he sighs in front of James. James looks at the ground and concentrates on the crisp and the crunch, but his ears perk at Luca’s sigh. Luca continues. “True, James. But here’s what I’ve been thinking.” He pauses and his next step lags. “I have trouble with organized religion. I’ve been trying to read the Bible, but it just doesn’t make sense to me. I don’t agree with certain parts of it, and I feel like I’m wrong for that.” James takes a puff from the cigarette. He’s lightheaded and a little nauseous but also curious what Luca’s wrong about. He asks while appreciating the cotton balls sprinkled into the sky around the sun. Luca responds. “I feel wrong for not believing in the Bible. It’s supposed to be written by God.”

James tones into Luca’s frequency, but it takes him a minute to ponder the concept. “Yes, but even if that’s true, it was translated by people. Over and over again until it got to English and into your hands. But, what do you not agree with from Christianity?” He hasn’t thought much about religion, only repeated things that they say in church and followed the general guidelines for what not to do. He’s shocked he never thought about it, but then shocked that he could have such a cohesive thought about it.

He doesn’t know how long Luca takes to respond, but his voice reminds James of the crunchy path filling his vision. “Well, like sex. The idea that it’s immoral to sleep with someone outside of marriage feels wrong to me. Marriage is an arbitrary and society-based standard to abide morals by. How could the creator of everything, how could God, base morality on a signed document? It feels as arbitrary to base love on marriage as it’s arbitrary to base intelligence on a degree.” Luca pauses. “And I’m not even sure if I believe it’s morally wrong to sleep with someone out of love. Why would God give us pleasure from sex if we weren’t supposed to enjoy it?”

James nods, even though he’s looking at Luca’s back. James snorts, even though it’s serious. “And why would God put the male g-spot in the ass if we weren’t supposed to do anal?” And then it isn’t serious. But it is to Luca: he doesn’t laugh.
James isn’t even sure if Luca listened to his ridiculous comment when he responds. “It makes me feel like there’s something immoral about me if I don’t agree with a religion, but I also don’t understand how one religion is more right than another. It’s very confusing when you’d go to hell in one religion and heaven in another for living the same life. How could God discriminate like that? How could we possibly know the right God?” James keeps looking at Luca’s back. His black puffy coat puffs like the cotton balls in the sky. “But if there’s one set of right morals, then we’d all have the same. If we all have the same morals at our core, then there should be some collective right set of morals. It’s most likely to be something that the majority of people believe in. I might just not agree with Christian morals because I’m so far away from reality in my mind.” He pauses again and James doesn’t know how to respond, but right before it gets awkward, Luca continues, “But the majority of people here are so different from the majority of people in, like, Saudi Arabia.”

James perks up while Luca broods that concept. “Well, like you said before, maybe morals depend on some mental evolution, so morals could have developed differently mentally in Saudi Arabia than over here.”

James can hear Luca’s smile in his voice. “Yeah, and each of us evolves mentally over our lifetime. My morality is based on different experiences than the people on the opposite side of the world.”

James chirps. “Maybe there can be different sets of morals that are each correct for different people.”

Luca adds. “Yeah, and God can understand the intricacies of morality for each person. God’s mind must be unlike our single minds that require patterns to understand large concepts or data.

Luca skips a step and his voice crunches like his steps. “Morality is nature-nurture.” His voice raises. “So I can be right not to believe the morals of a religion, and a person can also be right for believing in the religion.” James still has the cigarette and takes a pull. There’s hardly any left. He looks up to the back of Luca’s tilted-down head fixed on the ground.
James explores this new avenue of thoughts and the nicotine rushes to his head. A gentle incline evens out and the valley that contains the pond comes into view on the trail ahead. The yellow Hummer flickers distantly in the midday sun. James almost runs into Luca, stopped on the path in front of him. They stand there and nothing crunches.

Luca turns to James and pulls the cigarette packet out of his flannel pocket. His lighter falls into the snow. “Fuck.” Luca rasps, picking it up and blowing into the part that creates a flame, which is wet. He tries to light it and it takes a couple tries, each time with him blowing into it sharply. He cups the flame as he looks down his nose at the cigarette.
He reaches out a hand for James to take the cigarette. “Oh, thanks.” James mumbles, fumbling with the cigarette from his friend. He doesn’t want it, but takes it anyway. “What’s the point of religion? Like what’s religion supposed to do for us?” James asks. 

“It provides a community with the same moral compass, I guess. It provides a meaning to existence.”
“Yeah it gives life meaning, doesn’t it?” James frowns. “Well, it’s supposed to. If it’s supposed to provide meaning, then, yeah, I feel like the meaning of something could be totally different to two people. The meaning one person applies isn’t inherently wrong compared to another. Right?”

“Yes, James. Exactly. That makes sense in my head, and kind of relieves some stress at feeling immoral about not believing. But how do we assign meaning? Because it is certainly possible to apply the wrong meaning to something.” James takes a moment to respond, looking out over the snow-covered sagebrush field.

”What do you find meaning from?” James asks. Luca snorts and starts walking along the trail again.

Luca’s not-so-white shoes hop a small stream crossing. James slightly-whiter shoes hop right after. “I’m not sure, honestly. So far I’ve just found things that don’t provide meaning. The Bible of one of them. School is certainly another.” James turns to watch the trickling creek disappear under an ice sheet. He only notices that his shoe’s wet when it squishes under him. There’s no crunch, but the prickles of cold water over his foot feel crispy.

“Have you tried other religions? Maybe others would align more with your mind’s evolution.” James grimaces at the cold sloshing in his sock.

“I haven’t, really. I’m a bit afraid that none of them will speak to me and I’ll feel more wrong about that than only disagreeing with Christian values.”

James’ mind explodes with a million thoughts at once, and the lightheadedness leaking into his brain from the nicotine turns to a headache. He tries to organize his thoughts into a stream, focusing his ears on the white noise of the creek behind them, “Thinking about it, organized religion will never work for you. You don’t like people telling you what to do. Religion is based on everyone following along. The leaders tell everyone else what to think. You’ve never liked being told what to think. That doesn’t make you immoral. Or, at least it doesn’t seem to correspond to morality. If anything, the people that are listening to the leaders are likely more detached from reality than you. I mean, take a look at the Jim Jones clan.”
Luca snorts, and then coughs, because James made him snort mid-hit of his cigarette. “Wait, do you mean the ‘drink the Koolaid’ guy.” Luca splutters between coughs.

James doesn’t want the cigarette back, and he declines it, “Yes, the ‘drink the Koolaid’ guy. That was a brainwashing religion.”

Luca’s face shifts. “Yeah, but being yourself, and thinking for yourself, sucks, James.”

James doesn’t have much to say about that, so he looks at the scenery. Barren trees dribble with crusted snow in patches around the pond. Something moves in his peripheral and he turns to see a crow soar over a brightly stinging hill across the way. Zippers of clouds line the sky behind it.

They walk there with breath showing in the cold winter air, both enchanted by the landscape before them.

Luca whispers, almost too soft for James to hear him. “I feel meaning out here.”
