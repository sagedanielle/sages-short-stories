“It’s you!” I gasped at my red haired friend in the mirror above the sink.

She ran over to hug me with half-rubbed-in sunscreen on her tan cheeks. I set down one of my backpacks to reciprocate. One of the straps of her swimsuit poked me in the eye and I blinked as I pulled away. “It’s so great to see you again! What are the odds?” Her fiery blue eyes met mine before rushing back to the mirror to finish her sunscreen application.

I walked towards the bed written on my roomcard and threw my backpacks, one by one, onto my bottom bunk. “What a small, lovely world. When did you get here?” I looked out over a decrepit dwelling and along the white-rimmed skyline towards a far ocean world.

She glided out from the bathroom and sat on the bunk beside mine while I threw bags out of my backpack onto my bed until I grabbed one and opened it. “I think I’ve been here two days, but it’s hard to tell time here, isn’t it?” She ran her hands through her long hair and paused at the ends, inspecting them.

“Tell me about it, I’ve only been away from home for a-” I tapped my phone, “week, but it feels like ages.”

“Yes, and I want it to be forever. I’m going to the beach. You wanna come?” She looked at me with her lazy, sunkissed eyes. I looked back with my own.

“I was grabbing this, anyway.” I grinned and pulled out my own strapped swimsuit.
She dragged a beach towel from across her bunk and lay back to a fluff from her airy mattress. “Perfect. It’s so close.”

I pulled the curtains across my bed and sauntered towards the bathroom. “How do you like it here?” I shut the door of the bathroom until there was a crack and slipped off my shorts.

“Oh, it’s lovely. It’s heaven, really. The beach is beautiful and the night life is fun. We’ll have a blast.” I tied a blue top around my neck.

“Well, come along then.” I motioned to her thick red hair pooled around her head. She sat up with her towel and slid on sandals.

The hallway wound around a stairwell and we descended past a blonde-haired boy with brown eyes that lolled on my swimsuit and then hers behind me before lolling back to his path. “So how did you end up here? I literally just booked a flight the day before I left and booked this hostel because it’s the same as the one we stayed at in Bangkok.” I tripped a step to the landing on the ground floor. Lights stretched from our closest wall around the bend and when the other wall dissolved, a lazy pool game had its players leaning against tables with their pool sticks and glass bottles. The sun trickled under the canopy above them.

“Yeah, this one is so much better, though.” I looked from the glistening blue pool beyond the pool table to her nodding head.

“Dude, tell me about it.” We crossed the fun-having area to the bar and beyond it to a sun-sprinkled alleyway.

“Oh, but yeah, so I took the night bus.” I shook my head at her wide eyes. “It was an experience. I booked it like, same day. I can’t remember. But I heard good things about Phuket so here I am.” I paused as we walked around a bend in the alley road towards a towering five star hotel with its windows reflecting bright light. “Oh my god, though, dude, on the bus I thought there wasn’t a bathroom, so I stopped drinking water when they told me we wouldn’t stop until the morning and literally almost pissed myself until I hunted one down. It was like maybe two feet tall above the very rudimentary toilet and I thought I might fall in. But I succeeded with no disasters.”

“I honestly can’t believe you did that. It’s fourteen hours, right?” I nodded as we walked by a sign that said ‘laundry’- “Oh that reminds me, I have so much laundry to do. I’ll have to bring it down when we get back.” I nodded as I peered past the glass door that opened into a little garden with a dark-skinned woman chatting while hanging clothes idly from hangers.

“Yeah, it was ridiculous. But I’d never done it before so I had to at least once.” She laughed.

“Isn’t it barely cheaper than flying?” I nodded again and slipped sunglasses over my stinging eyes.

“Sure, but I tied my bag around my waist after someone tried to grab it. Bet you didn’t get that experience on a plane.” Her eyes widened.

Behind a tall metal fence, where the laundry shop had been before, tomb stones rose around succulents with hanging vines surrounding them. The trees sparkled in the sun. We walked to an intersection where a man yelled “taxi, taxi!” at us shaking our heads. And beyond that, the piece of heaven she talked about peeked out. The beach, behind a row of swaying palm trees, spread long and sparkly. We walked into the road without waiting for the cars and bikes to stop for us, which still concerned me. But the lazy afternoon loll spread through the cars and the woman selling her beach jewelry next to the path to the sand. My red haired friend skipped down to the beach with a grin on her tanned face, and I followed along the same way.

The beach wound around forested hills with white framed houses poking between them. I lay my purple towel in the sand and she did the same with hers. Wind kissed my hair from a vast turquoise ocean. She set her bag on her towel and slipped off her dress. I did the same and we sauntered into the sparkling water like a hug. The waves rolled around us as we stepped deeper, and we settled into shoulder height water. She dove under a wave and her long hair fell behind her back. Sparkly teeth shone in the sun.

She swam over and side-eyed me. “I didn’t tell you, but I hooked up with that Brit.”

“The one you kissed on the big screen?” I beamed remembering the blurry evening.

Her face tinted rosy in the bright sun. “Yeah. After you left, they put us on the screen again to finish our drinks.”

“Well, you guys had great chemistry.” I laughed.

“So funny to get set up by the cameras in a stadium.” I nodded and plunged under the surface for that warm salty water.

When I emerged, I scanned the beach for our bags. “Don’t worry, I’ve been keeping my eye on them.” She grinned and plunged under too.

The sun warmed my face and the buoyant sea comforted me. “Yeah, I left that night to see this Australian guy.” Her eyes lit up, so I continued. “We got a hotel room. I wasn’t sure if you guys would think I was a slut or something.” She looked intrigued and nonjudgemental. I twinkled. “It was so good. I spent the next day with him, the entire time in bed or close to it. We got a massage together, and went to this really cute place for dinner.” I paused looking out over a vast ocean. “I needed that.”

She bubbled. “Dude, I did the exact same shit with the Brit. We got a private room at the hostel that night.” I looked wide-eyed at her because she’d acted like she woke up in our hostel room, as did I. “It was so amazing. I also needed that. Sometimes life is just annoying and serious, and I needed something silly.” My fears evaporated looking at her rosy, lively face.

I slowed down in my head in that breezy water. We were just the same. And we were alive together in that lolling beach town. I dove under a little wave and we both started back to the shore at the same time. I looked at her from my towel on the cozy sand, and I thought we might be thinking the same thing too. “I want a beer.” She nodded and collected her items.

We, the two of us, the two of us beautiful and free, walked down the beach towards our inevitable fate of finding an ice cold beer. When we did, we sunk into beach chairs and slid sunglasses over our contented faces. The first swig was a breath of fresh air. I looked around at the people playing in the water and the groups playing soccer on the beach. They all had a muted haze on them, like the beach world mesmerized all our minds to settle. “Have you talked to him since?” She asked me from over her green-tinted sun glasses.

“No. Uh.” I paused, feeling embarrassed. “I don’t like him very much. I just wanted something easy and…”

“Yeah, girl, same. Sounds like we got what we wanted.” She clinked her bottle against mine. I grinned ear to ear and took another sip of the bubbling icy drink that cooled my worries.

“I want to do it again with someone else. And again and again. It made me relax. It made me less serious.” She nodded.

“Yes, same, but he’s been blowing up my phone. I don’t understand why he’s still texting me. It was fun for what it was, but I don’t want a boyfriend.”

The sun shone bright overhead, “Yeah, mine too. He offered to buy my ticket to go with him up north. He wanted to travel with me. I don’t know. I mean, I don’t want to deal with someone else’s emotions right now. I’m young and I came over to be by myself.”

“Men.” She said.

I snorted at that, and the sun settled like the rest of the world as my beer dwindled. A couple splashed and kissed in the water and it was the first time my mind didn’t feel resistance to that. It was the first time I accepted that didn't need to be a goal. I had more interesting goals like scuba diving and learning to fight.
