Until I walked along that road I didn't know I could be so good at something. The boys noticed. How could anyone not notice how well I walked that road? Overgrowth hung out into the road on one side, and on the other side the road curved around a rugged bend. The second side stretched out four lanes, and I made five lanes. My fifth lane was for walking. I made the fifth lane for me, and I made it for the boys. Those stupid boys.

Along the road, cars honked at me because they noticed how good I walked that road, too. They honked with their headlights in my eyes, but I knew that behind their blacked out windows they must have been cheering. They must have been shocked to see how good I walked that road. It surprised me the cars didn't pile up along their four lanes and try to join my fifth lane because my fifth lane stretched so superior to their lanes.

Around the bend more honks cheered me on. The boys caught up and asked me how I could possibly walk the road so well. It was miraculous, really. Who would have thought that me, little me, could walk the road that well? The boys voiced concern about how far down the road we would go, but I didn't know why that mattered.

I ran my hand through the overbrush dipping into my lane and wet leaves sprinkled my fingers. Telephone poles walked my road too, but they loomed far and misty beside me. You see, they were not as good at walking that road and the dark sky dulled their prowess. The boys reckoned I would walk the road forever. They pondered where I would get to in my fifth lane. I wondered where they cared to walk on that road, because I didn't care much at all. The overgrowth on one side enchanted me along it, and the cliff face on the other side carried my wandering eyes up the slope into an umbrella of comforting branches.

When we rounded the bend, my easily distracted mind drew to the flickering sign on a building up ahead. Symbols I couldn't discern curved and sloped above rows of foreign goods. I figured inspecting the side of the road was equally as important as walking along it, and just as I turned my heels in the gravel towards the crickets in the brush, the most perfect sight I had ever seen romped over to me.

I gasped the most breathless gasp at the most perfect pup that guarded the road I walked. The pup ran right into my outstretched arms, and I crumpled onto the gravel with him in my lap. Road walking became irrelevant. Who cares about walking a damn road when there's the most precious, perfect pup ever to grace the earth crumpled in your lap grasping for your hair? It was obvious that the universe had gifted me the most wonderful gift because I was so good at walking that road. The pup played with the ruffles of my dress and laid perfect puppy kisses on my hands.

The boys watched me, but of course they watched me because the most precious pup wanted to play with me. The most perfect pup rolled around in the gravel before me. God gifted me a little slice of heaven. One of the boys found the shop owner to ask where we were. He wanted to get home. I wanted to play with my pup. He asked the owner where a cab could go to. I played with the pup. He asked what the puppy's name was. I paused playing with the pup.

The boys burst out laughing. "Dolla?" One of them boomed at the other. "Tell 'eem we're at Dolla's shop." The shop owner shook her head and grabbed the phone from the boy's hand.

Their booming voices echoed through my head while I scratched behind the puppy's ears. He yawned and melted into my lap. "Dolla's a playa."

"Dolla knows what he wants."

"Dolla's the boss 'round here."

And it was the most true that Dollar, the most dazzling Dollar I had ever seen, would be the boss around there.

The boys dragged me away from my delightful Dollar and into a cab while I brushed the dirt off my dress. I looked at him, with his big curious eyes, and I wanted to put him in my pocket. But those foreign boys with their 'ride' and their 'hunger' made me leave that poor road and that poor pup. He looked so sad out the back window of the cab.

I could not leave my dreamy Dollar forever. I schemed how I would get back to that road. In fact, I schemed in the cab ride away from Dollar how I would get back to that buttery boy. 

I hopped on a bike and zoomed each road. However, I had no idea which road I had walked. The wind blew through my hair and over my face like cream, and overhead lanterns hung in lines, blinking in and out of my vision. The roads were wrong. They were busy. I drove them far until the lanterns blinked out of my periphery and my headlight became necessary. I drove until the roads cleared of pedestrians and the buildings became sparse. And then the telephone wires appeared and my path expanded to a four lane road.

"Ahah!" I yelled into the wind.

But the road stretched so long, and I didn't know how far to go.

I revved my bike and soared down the road with my dress flowing behind me. I was the best at driving that road. And I drove it and drove it, and I drove it, and then the engine sputtered. I looked down at my bike and found, that through all my meticulous attention on the side of the road for that one curve and that one foreign sign, that I had neglected to watch my gas level. I sputtered into a gas station and outturned my pockets to find, to my dismay, that they had no dollars.
