On my patio, the glass table vibrates and my heart jumps when I see the name with hearts next to it on my screen. I reach for it hoping she’s okay, but hesitate when my finger hovers over the green answer button. I take a deep breath of the calm summer air and click it. “Hi honey, are you- how are you doing? — it’s good to hear from you.”

A pause from the other end.

“Hi mom. Sorry I haven’t called lately. — I’m doing —  I’m doing fine. How are you?” The hesitancy in her voice makes me choke up and push it down my throat.

My breath shakes, and I put the phone away from my face so she doesn’t hear. “I’m good too. My hummingbird feeder lately has been attracting a lot of them. I love watching them flutter around. They’re so cute and I think of that time when you were little and you caught one out of the air. I couldn’t believe it back then. Your reflexes were so quick, still are I’m sure. There’s also a lot of ducks in the pond you used to paddleboard around on. I’ve been going down and…Well, really the same as every time you call. My life is pretty boring compared to yours.” My voice catches at the end of that, and I wish I had had a more significant college fund for her. I wondered what I could have done differently. I rambled. I don’t ramble with other people. “What have you been up to?” I want to know what my daughter has been up to so badly, but I also don’t know how I feel towards it whenever it comes into my peripheral vision. I wish I could look my daughter up online. My friends tiptoe around asking about her and the younger men at the grocery store look at me mischeviously. I push that from my mind. I want to know if it makes her happy.

I realize there’s been silence on the other end of the line while my mind has been going as fast as the hummingbird’s wings on the edge of my patio. She clears her throat. “I’ve made a lot of money recently. I can send some to you if you need any. Or if you want to treat yourself to anything.” Her voice is unstable like my brain. “I know you don’t need that.” She pauses and draws in a shaky breath. “Mom, I hate it. I want to be able to talk to you again. I’ve been questioning my decisions lately. I have all the money I could ever ask for. I did what I wanted and exceeded my expectations but I feel as hopeless as I did when I got that DUI. I thought I’d like this.” She pauses again and I feel some relief flood into me. “I don’t, mom. I don’t like how they treat me. I don’t feel like a person anymore. I did this because I didn’t feel like a person before, even when I pursued real estate people didn’t treat me much like one. I felt so fed up that I… I did what I set out to do and I feel worse than before. I’m not a person at all anymore, not even to those closest to me.” She sobbed through the phone, and I wanted to hug her hard enough to know that she was more.

”Come home, honey. The dogs miss you. I miss you. You don’t have to do that anymore.”

She paused for a long time while I held my breath. “Not today, mom.” And she spoke with conviction that always ended our phone calls. I wondered when her next call would come.
