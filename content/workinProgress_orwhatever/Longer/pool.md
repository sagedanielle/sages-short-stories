Slender, graceful fingers wrapped around my pool stick and pulled me from my obsession with the eight ball’s pocket. Soaked-blue eyes studied me. I hesitated to release the stick on my winning shot, but those long fingers wrapped so daintily around it only inches from my hardened ones. The release was thoughtless. My eyes traveled from her short, plain fingernails to the arch in her back when she leaned down to aim. Her stick swung up at the end and she missed miserably, but winning was obsolete. I only wanted to go wherever I could have her alone. I overlooked her high pitched whining because of the swing of her hips and she easily left with me guiding her. Along empty, street-lit streets, we wandered while she rambled. I nodded along automatically at loud nervous chatter about topics that didn’t interest me after she mentioned the royal English family. It was only then that I placed her loud, warbling accent. She rambled so much I studied the stucco sidewalk walls for slithering lizards. She rambled and rambled, and I nodded and considered that all good things have downsides. I wandered through doors below a “hotel” sign, and she followed without skipping a beat.

The man behind the counter regarded us with indifference and turned from his phone to his computer. The world lagged and blended until she pulled the cap off my head and slurred, with her overenunciated, big-toothed words, that I’m just like the hot rednecks from those American country films. I noted the comment rather joyously as I pulled her with a sly smirk into our room for the night. She melted into my hands when I ran them through her hair and whispered something dirty that had the dimly lit room soften around me. My prize for nodding along to her nonsense came slow and focused, and I kept my eyes trained on her soft skin and the curves of her frame. I put my hand over her mouth to keep her from running her loud, accented mouth.

I conquered the idiot so completely by early morning that I had to pry myself out of her possessive pale claws. She lay there limp when I stood over her.

Now, where did she put my hat?
